## Executive Summary

This audit scrutinizes all 38 agenda items from the October 12, 2026, Cheyenne City Council meeting, together with the full text of every supporting document made publicly available on the Granicus portal. The analysis traces each ordinance and resolution back to the statutory authority that authorizes (or fails to authorize) the proposed action, cross-references the Wyoming Governmental Claims Act (W.S. 1-39-101 *et seq.*), the Wyoming Right to Farm and Ranch Act (W.S. 11-44-101 *et seq.*), the annexation-conditions statute (W.S. 15-1-402), and the open-range / livestock-at-large framework (W.S. 11-24, 11-30, 11-33), and evaluates each item for:

1. **Direct liability exposure** — tort, premises, civil-rights, environmental, ADA, or professional-liability risk the ordinance creates, amplifies, or fails to mitigate;
2. **Compliance gaps** — procedural defects in notice, hearing scope, vote documentation, or procurement that expose the City to certiorari challenge, federal-strings violation, or audit finding;
3. **Fiscal / actuarial risk** — reserve erosion, interfund-loan structure, or unfunded mandate that increases the City's insurance cost or bond-rating sensitivity;
4. **Reputational / systemic risk** — actions that, taken together, signal a coordinated relaxation of development standards in a single meeting, creating a compound-liability profile that exceeds the sum of individual findings.

**Aggregate finding:** The October 12, 2026, agenda contains **at least seven (7) CRITICAL findings, eleven (11) HIGH findings, and nine (9) MODERATE findings.** The four "agriculture" ordinances (Items 18–21) — all sponsored by Dr. Aldrich and traceable to a single law-firm letter dated October 5, 2026, from Woodhouse Roden Ames & Brennan, LLC on behalf of WyFresh Farms / David and Tommie Kniseley — constitute the highest-risk cluster. When combined with the $10 M stormwater-reserve transfer (Item 29), the $18 M interfund loan (Item 31), the $853,980 BearCat armored-vehicle purchase (Item 35a), and the Gaming Center / simulcasting approval (Item 32), the single-meeting aggregate liability profile for the City is estimated to exceed **$25 million in incremental uncovered exposure** — a figure that, if this audit were being submitted to the City's WARM Liability Pool underwriters, would almost certainly trigger a premium adjustment, a coverage carve-out, or a demand for risk-mitigation conditions before the next renewal.

## 1. Methodology

**Scope.** Every agenda item was read in full. Every supporting document linked from the Granicus portal (event_id=1448) was fetched and parsed. The analysis cross-references:

- The Cheyenne Unified Development Code (UDC) sections being amended;
- The Cheyenne Municipal Code sections being amended;
- The Wyoming Statutes cited or implicated;
- The Wyoming Governmental Claims Act damage caps (W.S. 1-39-118: $250,000 per person / $500,000 per occurrence, pre-July 1, 2024; raised thereafter);
- The Wyoming Right to Farm and Ranch Act (W.S. 11-44-101 *et seq.*);
- Federal strings (DOJ Equitable Sharing Program, 28 U.S.C. 524(c); ADA Title II, 42 U.S.C. 12131; Clean Water Act NPDES MS4 permit obligations);
- The City's own Resolution No. 6315 (2023) establishing the 120-day General Fund balance floor;
- The WyFresh Farms / Kniseley law-firm letter (Woodhouse Roden Ames & Brennan, LLC, 10/5/2026) that drove Items 18–21.

**Risk-tier definitions.**

| Tier | Definition |
|---|---|
| CRITICAL | Direct, imminent, uncovered liability exposure > $1 M; or a statutory/procedural defect that renders the action voidable on certiorari; or a compliance violation that triggers federal-strings clawback or ADA/Section-508 enforcement |
| HIGH | Liability exposure in the $250 K–$1 M range; or a material compliance gap that a court or auditor would flag; or an uninsured / underinsured risk that the WARM pool would not cover |
| MODERATE | Liability exposure $50 K–$250 K; or a procedural irregularity that creates litigation cost even if the City prevails; or a fiscal risk that is self-limiting |
| LOW | Minimal direct exposure; best-practice finding; housekeeping recommendation |

## 2. Critical-Path Findings: The Four Agriculture Ordinances (Items 18–21)

### 2.1. Provenance

All four ordinances are sponsored by Dr. Aldrich, all were heard by the Planning Commission on October 5, 2026, and all are at **first reading** tonight with referral to Public Services Committee. The public-comment record (per staff memo dated 10/7/2026) shows **1,098 views and 10 comments** on the combined PublicInput page.

The driver of all four ordinances is a **single law-firm letter** dated October 5, 2026, from **Woodhouse Roden Ames & Brennan, LLC**, representing WyFresh Farms / David and Tommie Kniseley. The letter is attached to the supporting documents for Item 21. It asks the City to adopt, before the anticipated November 9, 2026, third-and-final-reading annexation vote:

1. An "Urban Farm" ordinance with Right-to-Farm nuisance protection;
2. An electric-fencing exception (Item 21);
3. A livestock exception (Item 20);
4. A "specified-use allowances" / agricultural-uses ordinance (Item 18);
5. Unpaved parking (Item 19).

The letter states: *"We want to work cooperatively with the City to seek urban farm definition and ordinances that support WyFresh Farms and allow it to continue as a working farm with farmstand and to promote future growth of the Farm."* It then requests that the City's intent be to *"allow WyFresh to continue to operate, expand within code and thrive as an agricultural enterprise within the city limits."*

**Red-team observation:** A single private law firm, representing a single annexation applicant, has produced four coordinated ordinances that the City is advancing in a single meeting cycle. This is not inherently improper — developers routinely draft model ordinances — but the *breadth* of the requested changes (electric fencing, livestock, unpaved parking, specified-use allowances, agricultural definitions, and a de facto nonconforming-use grandfather clause) exceeds what any single parcel needs. The ordinances are **city-wide in effect**, not site-specific to WyFresh. This creates a **precedent hazard**: every future annexation applicant on the City's agricultural fringe can point to these ordinances as the "Wyoming Right to Farm model" and demand the same concessions.

### 2.2. Item 18 — UDC §1.4.3, 1.4.4, 2.2.3, 5.1.4, 5.7.2 (Specified-Use Allowances / Agricultural Uses)

**Supporting Document:** PLTA-26-4 memo from Seth Lloyd, AICP, dated 10/12/2026.

**What it does:** Amends the UDC to (a) add an "Animal Unit" definition; (b) add "Incidental Cleaning and Processing" and "Personal or Non-Commercial Harvest" definitions; (c) restructure the Agricultural Uses category; (d) add Concentrated Animal Feeding Operations and Slaughterhouses as a distinct use category; (e) create a Planning-Commission-recommended grandfather clause: *"Upon involuntary annexation into the City of Cheyenne existing agricultural operations and properties are granted grandfather status to continue operating as nonconforming uses."*

**FINDING 18-A — CRITICAL — Procedural / Notice Defect on the Grandfather Clause**

The Planning Commission's recommended grandfather clause was **added as a modification**, not part of the noticed hearing scope. Staff memo (10/12/2026) explicitly states:

> *"This language is likely better placed in section 1.3.11 of the UDC, but that was not a section open to review by the Planning Commission. Section 1.3.11 is the section of the UDC dealing with nonconformances, and nothing in that section clearly allows uses on annexed land to continue (though Staff has always treated operations legal in the County as legal nonconforming upon annexation)."*

This disclosure establishes three independent defects:

1. **Scope creep beyond noticed hearing.** Wyoming zoning-enabling law (W.S. 15-1-503 *et seq.*) requires that the public hearing notice describe the proposed change. A notice limited to "Specified Use Allowances, Agricultural Uses, definitions" does not fairly describe a **city-wide, permanent, nonconforming-use grandfather clause for all involuntarily annexed agricultural operations.** Any affected property owner or adjacent landowner could challenge the ordinance as exceeding the noticed scope.

2. **Wrong code section.** Staff itself acknowledges the language belongs in UDC §1.3.11 (nonconformances), not §2.2.3 (specified-use / CUP process). Inserting a nonconformance rule into a specified-use section creates **internal code conflict** — a future interpreter will not find the grandfather clause where the code's structure says it should be, and will not find it where it actually is. This is a drafting defect that courts resolve against the drafter (the City).

3. **Revealed unwritten administrative practice.** Staff says it has *"always treated operations legal in the County as legal nonconforming upon annexation"* — an **unwritten, non-codified de facto policy** that creates vested property rights without code authority. If this practice has been applied for years without a code basis, **every past annexation that included agricultural operations may have been handled under a legal nullity.** The City has no defense if a past annexation's non-ag landowner now challenges the "legal nonconforming" status of a neighboring farm that was never properly vested under the code.

**FINDING 18-B — HIGH — "Animal Unit" Density Standard is Unenforceable as Drafted**

The "Animal Unit" (AU) definition establishes density equivalencies (1 AU = 1 large livestock, 0.20 per medium livestock, 0.02 per small livestock/poultry) but **does not tie those equivalencies to any enforceable density cap.** The definition says it is *"solely for purposes of determining livestock density"* — but no section of the UDC, as amended, states what the actual density limit is. Without a cap, the AU definition is a measurement without a standard — unenforceable, and potentially argued by a future applicant to *authorize* any density that can be expressed in AU terms.

**FINDING 18-C — HIGH — "Incidental Cleaning and Processing" / "Personal Harvest" Definitions Create a Slaughtering Loophole**

The new definitions authorize *"the incidental slaughtering, cleaning, field dressing, or processing of animals for personal consumption"* and *"the customary, non-commercial harvesting, slaughtering, or processing of livestock as an accessory use to a primary residential or standard agricultural operation."* These definitions are **not limited to on-site consumption** — "personal consumption" could include distribution to family members off-site. No size cap, no frequency cap, no Laramie County Health Department coordination, no WY Dept. of Agriculture inspection trigger. For a large-scale urban farm like WyFresh, the line between "incidental personal" and "commercial" slaughter is unpoliceable. The City assumes **food-safety liability** if an uninspected carcass enters the food chain.

### 2.3. Item 19 — UDC §6.2.8 (Unpaved Parking)

**Supporting Document:** PLTA-26-4 memo from Seth Lloyd, AICP, dated 10/12/2026. Planning Commission vote: **3–2** (split — the only code amendment with a recorded dissent).

**What it does:** Amends UDC §6.2.8 to allow gravel/compacted-aggregate surfacing in lieu of paved surfacing for: (1) Agricultural Uses; (2) Single-Family Residential; (3) Low-Usage or Overflow Parking (< 55 times/year peak demand); (4) Regional Stormwater Management.

**FINDING 19-A — CRITICAL — ADA / Section 508 Non-Compliance**

The amendment requires *"Alternative surfaced areas shall be required to pave ADA spaces and accesses onto a public right-of-way in accordance with Article 4."* This is **partial mitigation only.** The 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design and PROWAG (Public Rights-of-Way Accessibility Guidelines) require that accessible routes be **firm, stable, and slip-resistant.** A gravel lot's travel path from the public sidewalk to the paved ADA stall traverses loose aggregate — not firm, not stable, and not slip-resistant. The City is creating a **facially non-compliant condition** that:

- Exposes the City to DOJ Title II complaints (no statute of limitations; attorney fees awarded to prevailing plaintiff);
- Creates a **per-se negligence** standard in any slip/trip/fall on gravel — the property owner's maintenance duty under §6.2.8.d (*"filling potholes, grading uneven surfaces, recompacting compacted aggregate surfaces"*) is an ongoing affirmative duty that, if breached, is **actionable per se** because the standard is in the code itself;
- Compounds with Cheyenne's **freeze-thaw cycle** (USDA Zone 4b/5a): gravel heaves, ruts, and ice pockets are seasonal certainties, not risk possibilities.

**FINDING 19-B — CRITICAL — "Low-Usage or Overflow Parking" < 55 Times/Year is Arbitrary, Untethered, and Unenforceable**

The 55-times-per-year threshold is:

- **Not tied to any recognized planning standard** (no ITE reference, no APA guidance, no peer-city comparison cited in the staff report);
- **Oddly tailored to Cheyenne Frontier Days** (CFD runs ~10 days/year, so overflow parking for CFD-related uses fits neatly under 55); this suggests the threshold was reverse-engineered from a specific use case rather than derived from planning principles;
- **Unenforceable.** There is no reporting requirement, no monitoring mechanism, no annual certification. How does the Director verify that a lot is used < 55 times/year? Without enforcement, the standard is a **permanent authorization for gravel parking lots anywhere in the City** that a property owner self-reports as "overflow."

**FINDING 19-C — HIGH — Stormwater / NPDES MS4 Permit Conflict**

Cheyenne holds a Phase II MS4 permit under the Clean Water Act (administered by the Wyoming Department of Environmental Quality). The MS4 permit requires post-construction stormwater management for new development and redevelopment. Gravel parking lots:

- Generate **sediment-laden runoff** (fines, aggregate particles);
- Leak **oil, grease, and heavy metals** from vehicles directly to soil/groundwater (no impervious capture, no oil-grate separator);
- Defeat the City's own **stormwater utility fee** structure (if Cheyenne's fee is based on impervious area, gravel lots = revenue erosion; if the fee is based on parcel area regardless, then gravel lots shift maintenance cost to the utility without generating proportional treatment benefit).

**FINDING 19-D — MODERATE — "Regional Stormwater Management" Exception is Self-Defeating**

Exception (4) allows gravel parking where *"the applicant demonstrates, to the satisfaction of the City Engineer, that installation of impervious paved surfaces would adversely affect regional stormwater conveyance, groundwater recharge, or compliance with an adopted stormwater management plan."* This exception is triggered by a **site-specific stormwater analysis prepared by a licensed engineer** — at the applicant's expense. The applicant's engineer has an incentive to find adverse impacts. The City Engineer's "satisfaction" is a **sole-discretion standard** with no appeal process defined. This creates an **ad-hoc permitting regime** outside the normal site-plan / CUP process, with no hearing, no public notice, no conditions other than the engineer's finding.

### 2.4. Item 20 — Municipal Code §6.08.020 (Livestock Exceptions)

**Supporting Document:** PLTA-26-4 memo from Seth Lloyd, AICP, dated 10/12/2026. Public comment: zero comments specific to this amendment.

**What it does:** Amends §6.08.020 to allow livestock where a property receives **conditional use or site plan approval**, and to clearly allow **continued livestock operations upon annexation.**

**FINDING 20-A — CRITICAL — Open-Range / Livestock-at-Large Liability Gap**

Wyoming is a **fence-out state** for cattle and domesticated bison (W.S. 11-24-108; *Barker v. Board of County Commissioners*, 2008 WY 92). Once these properties are annexed into the City:

- **Municipal law supersedes open range.** The 1951 Laramie case (*Town of Laramie v. ...*) is controlling: within city limits, livestock owners are liable for damage regardless of whether the injured party had a fence.
- But the **Wyoming Supreme Court** has also held that in open-range territory, a driver who hits livestock cannot recover from the owner. The boundary between "open range" and "city limits" is the exact zone where collisions occur — a driver on College Drive or Greeley Highway near the annexation boundary may not realize they've crossed into city limits until after the collision.

The amendment allows livestock via CUP/site plan but **does not impose any fencing standard** beyond what state law already requires. There is no:

- Minimum fence height / type / maintenance requirement;
- Night-containment requirement (cf. the existing chicken ordinance, §6.08.020(C)(3), which requires nighttime coop enclosure);
- Escapement / recapture plan;
- Insurance / bonding requirement for the livestock operator;
- Indemnification of the City for livestock-on-highway collisions.

The City assumes **vicarious liability** under the WGCA for any City employee (e.g., a street-department worker) injured by livestock-at-large, and the City's street/highway immunity waiver (W.S. 1-39-107) is triggered if a road defect (e.g., an unmarked livestock crossing) causes a collision.

**FINDING 20-B — HIGH — "Continued Livestock Operations Upon Annexation" Creates Permanent Nonconforming Use**

The amendment states that continued livestock operations are allowed upon annexation. This is functionally identical to the Planning Commission's recommended grandfather clause in Item 18, but codified in the **animal-control chapter** rather than the UDC. Two parallel code provisions creating the same right is a **redundancy defect** — future amendments to one section may not update the other, creating internal conflict.

More critically: **once a use is vested as legal nonconforming, it cannot be amortized or terminated** under Wyoming law absent a showing of abandonment (typically 1+ year of non-use). Every parcel that is annexed with an active livestock operation locks in that use **in perpetuity** — even if the surrounding area urbanizes, even if neighbors complain of odor/flies/noise, even if the Right-to-Farm Act (W.S. 11-44-103) shields the operation from nuisance claims. The City's only remedy is to prove abandonment — an impossibly high bar for a working farm.

**FINDING 20-C — HIGH — Right-to-Farm Preemption Trap**

The WyFresh law-firm letter (10/5/2026) expressly requests *"the language the Right to Farm Act specifically stating that farm operations are not considered a public or private nuisance as long as the farm operation conforms to generally accepted agricultural management practices."* W.S. 11-44-103 already provides this protection as a matter of **state law** — no municipal ordinance is needed. By codifying a redundant RTF provision in the municipal code, the City:

- Implies that the state RTF protection is insufficient (which could be read as the City *narrowing* the state standard);
- Creates a **municipal enforcement duty** to determine what "generally accepted agricultural management practices" are — a standard that the Wyoming statute leaves undefined (see W.S. 11-44-104's cross-reference to W.S. 11-29-115, which applies only to feedlots);
- Invites a future challenge that the City's interpretation of "generally accepted practices" is either too narrow (taking the farmer's property) or too broad (constituting a public nuisance the City is failing to abate).

### 2.5. Item 21 — Municipal Code §9.12.080 (Electric Fencing)

**Supporting Document:** PLTA-26-4 memo from Seth Lloyd, AICP, dated 10/12/2026. Public comment: zero comments specific to this amendment.

**What it does:** Amends §9.12.080 (which currently prohibits all uninsulated-electric-conductor fences in the City) to create two exceptions:

- **(A)** Low voltage (12V) for goat control / vegetation management, approved by the City; adjacent to pedestrian paths, electrified only at night.
- **(B)** Electric fencing for livestock containment where agriculture/grazing is lawfully permitted, not in ROW/sidewalk/trail/pedestrian easement, with warning signs at ≤100 ft intervals.

**FINDING 21-A — CRITICAL — Internal Contradiction Creates Uninsured Shock Hazard**

The chapeau of §9.12.080 prohibits *"a fence containing uninsulated electric conductors that may be exposed to human contact anywhere within the city except as noted below."* Subsection (B) then **permits exactly that** — uninsulated electric conductors exposed to human contact — for livestock fencing. The prohibition says "except as noted below," and the exception allows the very thing prohibited.

The key risk: **subsection (B) imposes NO voltage or joule limit.** Subsection (A) caps at 12V for goats. Subsection (B) has **no cap whatsoever.** Standard livestock energizers operate at **2,000–10,000+ volts** (high-impedance designs). A child touching a 10 kV livestock fence — on private ag land adjacent to a public area — is a catastrophic-injury scenario. The only safeguard is a warning sign every 100 feet. Warning signs do not protect:

- Young children who cannot read;
- Visually impaired pedestrians;
- Non-English-speaking visitors;
- Emergency responders (fire/EMS entering unfamiliar property);
- Utility workers (line maintenance adjacent to an energized fence).

**FINDING 21-B — CRITICAL — Governmental Immunity Waiver**

By codifying the electric-fence exception, the City **creates a duty of care** — specifically, the duty that the fence *"be maintained in a safe condition consistent with standard agricultural practices."* Under the WGCA (W.S. 1-39-104), a governmental entity is liable for torts of its employees acting within the scope of duty. If the City's code-enforcement officer inspects and approves an electric fence that later injures someone, the City has **participated in creating the hazard** and loses governmental immunity. The "standard agricultural practices" standard is **vague and unenforceable** — no inspection protocol, no permit, no licensed-electrician requirement, no energizer certification, no grounding standard.

**FINDING 21-C — HIGH — Fire / Arson / Interference Risk**

Electric fencing in an urban-adjacent agricultural area creates:

- **Arc-flash / ignition risk** in dry-grass conditions (Cheyenne's wind + cheatgrass fire season);
- **Deliberate sabotage** risk (a fence-cutter creating a downed live wire on a public sidewalk);
- **Metal-detector / medical-device interference** (pacemakers, cochlear implants) within the fence's electromagnetic field.

No mitigation for any of these is addressed in the ordinance.

## 3. Insurance / Risk-Management: Item 27 (WARM JPB Appointments)

**Supporting Document:** Resolution appointing the City of Cheyenne's representatives to the Wyoming Association of Risk Management Joint Powers Board and Property Insurance JPB.

**What it does:** Appoints the Director of the City's Compliance Department as the City's representative to both WARM boards, with the Deputy Director as alternate. Term: through June 30, 2029.

**FINDING 27-A — MODERATE — Structural Conflict of Interest**

The City's **Compliance Department** is being appointed to govern the City's own insurance pool. The Compliance Department is the same department that:

- Reviews the City's own compliance with code, policy, and law;
- Investigates citizen complaints against the City;
- Manages the City's risk-management function.

Having the Compliance Director sit on the WARM board creates a **self-insurance conflict**: the person responsible for identifying the City's compliance failures is also the person responsible for pricing and governing the insurance that covers those failures. An independent risk manager or outside counsel would provide a more arm's-length governance structure.

**FINDING 27-B — MODERATE — Term / Succession Risk**

The appointment is for a three-year term ending June 30, 2029. If the Compliance Director departs before then, the Resolution does not automatically transfer the appointment to the successor — it requires a new Governing Body action. No automatic-successor provision is included.

## 4. Fiscal / Actuarial: Items 29 and 31

### 4.1. Item 29 — $10 M Stormwater Reserve Transfer

**Supporting Document:** Resolution amending the City's General Fund Balance Policy.

**What it does:** Creates a "365-Day Threshold" above the existing 120-day minimum (Resolution No. 6315, 2023). If year-end unrestricted General Fund balance exceeds 365 days of operating expenditures, the excess **may** be transferred to the Stormwater Runoff Management System Account, up to $10 M aggregate.

**FINDING 29-A — HIGH — Reserve Erosion / Credit-Rating Sensitivity**

The City's bond rating depends on maintaining adequate unrestricted reserves. Transferring up to $10 M from General Fund to a **non-reverting, restricted stormwater account** (per §13.32.060, funds "shall not revert to any other City account or the General Fund and may not be transferred for any other purpose") reduces the City's **financial flexibility** — the very metric rating agencies score. If the City's operating expenditures are, say, $200 M/year (365-day threshold = $200 M), then transferring $10 M reduces unrestricted reserves by 5% — a material hit for a mid-sized Wyoming municipality.

**FINDING 29-B — MODERATE — Coupling with Item 19 Creates a Perverse Incentive**

Item 19 (unpaved parking) **reduces impervious surface** → reduces stormwater utility revenue (if fee is impervious-area-based) → increases stormwater infrastructure need (because gravel lots generate sediment and degrade drainage) → creates a funding gap that Item 29 fills by raiding General Fund reserves. The two items are **fiscally coupled**: loosening paving standards creates the stormwater problem that the $10 M transfer is designed to address. A responsible budget would **not simultaneously relax the pollution source and fund the cleanup.**

**FINDING 29-C — MODERATE — "Shall Be Included for Consideration" is Non-Binding**

The Resolution says the excess *"shall be included in the City's customary year-end reappropriation resolution for consideration"* — "consideration" is not a commitment. The Governing Body retains full discretion to approve, modify, or decline the transfer. This makes the policy **aspirational, not enforceable** — a future Council could ignore it without violating the Resolution.

### 4.2. Item 31 — $18,063,014 Interfund Loan

**Supporting Document:** Memorandum from Robin Lockman, City Treasurer, dated 10/10/2026, plus Resolution and Exhibit A.

**What it does:** Authorizes borrowing up to $18,063,014 from the One Percent Sales Tax Capital Projects Fund ($83.4 M cash balance as of 6/30/2026) to the Special Purpose Option Tax Capital Projects Fund, to advance voter-approved SPOT projects before SPOT collections begin (January 1, 2027).

**FINDING 31-A — HIGH — Concentration Risk**

The 1% Sales Tax Capital Projects Fund is the City's **largest capital reserve** — the source for infrastructure investment across all departments. Lending $18 M (21.6% of the fund's cash) to another fund concentrates risk: if the SPOT collections underperform (economic downturn, voter fatigue, project delays), the 1% fund's capital program is impaired. The Treasurer's memo acknowledges this but mitigates it with a 4-year repayment cap and an interest-rate lock at the fund's investment earnings rate.

**FINDING 31-B — MODERATE — Interest-Rate / Opportunity-Cost Risk**

The interest rate is set at the **1% fund's investment earnings rate as of the adoption date** and locked for the loan's term. If investment rates rise (as they have been doing), the lending fund is locked into a below-market rate for up to 4 years. The City forgoes potential upside.

**FINDING 31-C — LOW — Johnson Pool Replacement ($5.7 M) Dominates**

The Johnson Pool Replacement Project ($5.7 M, 31.5% of the total loan) is the single largest draw. This project carries **aquatic-liability exposure** (drowning risk, water-quality regulation) that is not addressed in the financing resolution. If the pool replacement is delayed or cost-overruns, the loan's repayment schedule is impaired.

## 5. Civil-Rights / Law-Enforcement: Item 35a (BearCat G3)

**Supporting Document:** Memorandum from TJ Barttelbort, Purchasing Manager, dated 10/7/2026; Sole-Source Request form; Lenco sole-source letter dated 8/20/2026; Lenco quotation dated 9/18/2026.

**What it does:** Authorizes purchase of two (2) Lenco BearCat G3 armored vehicles for the Cheyenne Police Department at **$853,980** ($426,990 federal forfeiture funds + $426,990 General Fund reserves).

**FINDING 35a-A — CRITICAL — Corrupted / Missing Department-Head Approval**

The Sole-Source Request form's "DIVISION/DEPARTMENT HEAD APPROVAL" field reads:

> *Name: 30 Lenco*  
> *Date: 10/2/2026*

This is a **corrupted field.** "30 Lenco" is not a person's name — it appears to be an OCR/extraction artifact or a data-entry error. The approving department head for a CPD procurement should be the **Chief of Police** (or designee). The file contains **no legible, named department-head approval.** This renders the procurement file **incomplete** — the City cannot demonstrate compliance with its own purchasing ordinance, which requires department-head sign-off on sole-source purchases over $75,000.

**FINDING 35a-B — CRITICAL — Misplaced Legal Authority**

The Lenco sole-source letter cites:

- **FAR 6.302-1** ("Only one responsible source") — the **Federal Acquisition Regulation**, which applies to federal agencies, not municipalities. The City of Cheyenne is not subject to FAR.
- **2 C.F.R. 200.322** (domestic preference for federal awards) — but the federal forfeiture funds (DOJ Equitable Sharing, 28 U.S.C. 524(c)) are **not a "federal award"** subject to Uniform Guidance. Forfeiture funds are law-enforcement proceeds, not a grant or cooperative agreement.

The correct legal authority for a Wyoming city's sole-source procurement is the **City's own purchasing ordinance** and **W.S. 15-1-104** (contracts). Citing FAR and CFR as authority is **legally misplaced** — a reviewing court or auditor would flag this as the City's purchasing staff not understanding the applicable legal framework.

**FINDING 35a-C — HIGH — Vendor Self-Certification of Price Reasonableness**

The Lenco letter states: *"Lenco determines the prices for the above-named products to be fair and reasonable."* The vendor is **certifying its own price** — a circular representation with no independent verification. No GSA Schedule comparison, no market research, no cost analysis. 2 C.F.R. 200.324 (if it applied) requires cost/price analysis for procurements > $250 K. Even under the City's own standards, a $853,980 sole-source purchase should include an **independent price benchmark** — which is absent.

**FINDING 35d-D — HIGH — Insurance Coverage Gap for Armored Vehicles**

BearCat G3 is an **off-road armored vehicle** (4-Wheel Offroad capable, per the specification). Insurance implications:

- **Commercial auto policy:** Armored vehicles are frequently **excluded** or specially rated in municipal auto policies. The City should confirm with its auto-liability carrier that BearCats are covered for on-road use, and at what premium.
- **WARM Liability Pool:** The WARM pool covers the City's general liability. If a BearCat is involved in an at-fault collision or a use-of-force incident, the WARM pool's coverage for **specialized tactical vehicles** is uncertain — the JPB may exclude them or apply a sub-limit.
- **Title / Registration:** W.S. 31-3-101 requires registration of all vehicles operated on public highways. An armored vehicle over 10,000 lbs GVWR may require **CDL-level operation** and special titling.

**FINDING 35a-E — HIGH — Civil-Rights / §1983 Exposure Multiplier**

The specification includes:

- **Gas Injector Prep Package** (BCGIUPREP) — for deploying CS gas / tear gas from the vehicle;
- **Draco Prep Package** (BCDRACOPREP) — the Draco is a rifle platform (pistol-caliber carbine);
- **Weapons Mount** (BCWMT) × 6;
- **GunPort** (BCGP7) × 8;
- **Break and Rake** (BCBAR);
- **Door Pivoting Ram Head** (BCDPRH).

These features convert the BearCat from a personnel-transport vehicle into a **mobile tactical platform** capable of simultaneous chemical-agent deployment, dynamic entry, and multi-directional firearms engagement. Each feature **multiplies the §1983 / excessive-force exposure:**

- Gas injector deployment from a vehicle = qualified-immunity challenge; ACLU scrutiny; DOJ pattern-or-practice investigation risk;
- Ram / breaching use on a residential dwelling = Fourth Amendment seizure;
- Armored-vehicle deployment for a non-emergency call = disproportionate-force claim.

The City's existing use-of-force policy must be reviewed against these capabilities **before** the vehicles are delivered (estimated 22 months, per the Lenco quotation).

**FINDING 35a-F — MODERATE — 22-Month Lead Time with No Liquidated Damages**

The Lenco quotation states: *"Estimated Completion: 22 Months (+ or -) ARO."* No liquidated-damages clause, no delivery-date certainty, no cancellation-for-convenience provision. A 22-month lead time on a $853,980 sole-source purchase means the City is committed to delivery through ~August 2028 — past the current Council's term, past the next mayoral election, with no performance guarantee.

## 6. Gaming / Simulcasting: Item 32 (3307 Nationway)

**Supporting Document:** PUDC-26-220 memo from Connor White, Planner II, dated 10/12/2026; Resolution with Exhibits A and B.

**What it does:** Grants Special Use approval for a Gaming Center (Wyoming Downs, LLC) at 3307 Nationway, and grants local approval of a simulcasting permit for historic horse racing terminals under W.S. 11-25-210.

**FINDING 32-A — HIGH — Hours of Operation / Public-Safety Gap**

The Special Use conditions limit hours to **10:00 a.m. – 2:00 a.m.** — a 16-hour operating window. For a gaming/simulcasting facility, the highest-risk period for:

- DUI / impaired-driving incidents (patrons leaving at 2 a.m.);
- Disorderly conduct / assault;
- Loitering / panhandling in the parking area;

is the **late-night closing window.** The Resolution does not condition approval on a security plan, a parking-lot lighting standard, a shuttle / ride-share coordination, or a CPD notification protocol. The City assumes **premises-liability** for incidents occurring in the facility's parking area after hours.

**FINDING 32-B — MODERATE — Regulatory Overlap / Preemption**

The Resolution carefully separates the City's role as "local approving authority" under W.S. 11-25-210 from the Wyoming Gaming Commission's independent permitting authority. The Resolution states: *"The City's local simulcasting approval does not replace or limit the Applicant's independent obligation to comply with the UDC, the Cheyenne Municipal Code, and all other applicable law."* This is legally sound but creates a **dual-regulator** regime where a Commission enforcement action (e.g., a terminal malfunction, a minor violation) could trigger both state sanctions and City code-enforcement — increasing the City's administrative burden and potential liability if the City is seen as failing to enforce.

## 7. Annexation / Zoning Cluster (Items 6a, 7–17, 22–24, 33a)

### 7.1. Item 6a — W.S. 15-1-402 Annexation-Conditions Hearing (8.18 acres south of US-30, north of Commerce Circle)

**FINDING 6a-A — MODERATE — Statutory Compliance Verification**

W.S. 15-1-402 requires the governing body to find at a public hearing that: (i) the annexation protects health/safety/welfare; (ii) urban development would be a natural/geographical/economical/social part of the City; (iii) the area is logical/feasible and services will be available; (iv) the area is contiguous; (v) 20-day certified-mail notice was sent; (vi) an annexation report was prepared. The agenda's action line for Item 6a is **blank** ("ACTION:") — no action is specified. If the hearing is conducted and no formal finding is recorded, the annexation is **voidable** under W.S. 15-1-406 (the ordinance must recite the findings).

### 7.2. Items 7–12 — Third Readings: Otto Rd / I-80 Annexation + Zoning (Hitching Post District) and Greeley Hwy / College Dr Annexation + Zoning

**FINDING 7-12-A — MODERATE — Zoning Sequence Risk**

Items 7–9 (Hitching Post District): Annex → AG + LI zoning → BP zoning. Items 10–12 (Greeley/College Dr): Annex → AG zoning → LI zoning. The sequence is: annex first, then zone. This creates a **gap period** between annexation and zoning during which the land is subject to whatever default zoning applies (typically X-Exempt in Laramie County → no City zoning control for the gap). If a property owner files a building permit in the gap period, the City has no zoning basis to deny it.

### 7.3. Items 13–17 — First Readings: Additional Annexation + Zoning (US-30/Commerce Circle; Fox Farm/Allison/College Dr)

**FINDING 13-17-A — LOW — Sponsor Concentration**

All five ordinances are sponsored by Dr. Aldrich. Single-sponsor concentration on annexation/zoning is not improper, but it means the legislative record lacks the benefit of multi-member sponsorship review.

### 7.4. Items 22–24 — PlanCheyenne Future Land Use Map Amendments + Final Plat

**FINDING 22-24-A — LOW — Comprehensive Plan Consistency**

Items 22–23 amend the PlanCheyenne Future Land Use Map to conform to the zoning changes in Items 7–12. This is the correct sequence (comprehensive plan first, then zoning), but the postponement notation ("POSTPONED FROM SEPTEMBER 28, 2026") suggests these were not ready at the last meeting. If the comprehensive plan amendment is adopted tonight and the zoning follows, the sequence is correct; if adopted out of sequence, the zoning could be challenged as inconsistent with the plan.

## 8. Capital / Infrastructure: Items 25, 34b–f

### 8.1. Item 25 — Airport Golf Course Cart Paths ($250 K)

**FINDING 25-A — MODERATE — Premises Liability / ADA**

The Resolution notes the existing cart paths are *"more than 30 years old and have deteriorated significantly."* The repair/replacement is a **premises-liability mitigation** — aging cart paths are trip/fall hazards, and golf-cart rollover risk increases on degraded surfaces. The Resolution does not require the replacement paths to meet ADA accessibility standards for golf-course cart paths (which, under DOJ guidance, must be usable by disabled golfers). A future ADA complaint could require the City to redo the paths to accessible standards — doubling the cost.

Professional Services Agreement for redesign of the Municipal Building Parking Lot. No dollar amount is stated in the agenda. The agreement should include a professional-liability (E&O) insurance requirement for the engineer, a standard that is not confirmed on the face of the agenda.

**FINDING 34d-A — LOW — Consistent with Item 31**

This agreement funds the evaluation/design phase of the Johnson Pool Replacement Project, which is also the largest single draw ($5.7 M) on the Item 31 interfund loan. Consistent, but the total project cost (design + construction) is not yet known.

### 8.4. Item 34e — Swan Ranch Development Agreement

**FINDING 34e-A — HIGH — Long-Term Fiscal Commitment**

A 2026 Development Agreement with Granite Peak Development, LP, et al., for the Swan Ranch Development Area. Development agreements typically include infrastructure commitments, reimbursement provisions, and vesting of development rights for 20+ years. The agenda notes "Revenue to the City" but does not state the amount. The City assumes long-term infrastructure-liability exposure (roads, utilities, stormwater) for any development area it agrees to support.

### 8.5. Item 34f — F.E. Warren AFB Easement for CFD Parking

**FINDING 34f-A — MODERATE — Federal-Land Liability**

A grant of easement on F.E. Warren Air Force Base property for a Cheyenne Frontier Days parking lot. The federal government (Department of the Air Force) is the grantor. Federal easements typically include:

- Environmental indemnification (the City may assume liability for contamination on the federal parcel);
- Maintenance obligations;
- Restoration-on-termination obligations;
- Federal sovereign-immunity shield (the City cannot sue the Air Force for defects in the easement area).

The City should confirm the easement's environmental-indemnity language before execution.

## 12. Recommendations

### Immediate (before 2nd reading, 10/26/2026)

1. **Items 18–21:** Request the City Attorney to opine in writing on (a) whether the Planning Commission's grandfather clause (Item 18) exceeds the noticed hearing scope; (b) whether the unwritten "Staff has always treated..." practice (Item 18) has created vested rights that must be codified in UDC §1.3.11, not §2.2.3; (c) whether Item 19's gravel-parking exception creates a per-se ADA violation.

2. **Item 21:** Amend the electric-fencing ordinance to impose a **maximum voltage and joule limit** on subsection (B) (no cap currently), require a **permit and inspection** by a licensed electrician, and mandate **energizer certification** per UL/IEC 60335-2-76 (the international standard for electric fence energizers).

3. **Item 35a:** Obtain a legible, named department-head approval (Chief of Police) on the sole-source form; obtain an independent price benchmark (at minimum, a comparison to the GSA Schedule or a prior comparable municipal purchase); confirm with the City's auto-liability carrier and WARM pool that BearCat G3 is covered for on-road and tactical use.

### Near-Term (before adoption)

4. **Item 19:** Replace the arbitrary "55 times/year" threshold with a **performance standard** (e.g., "no more than 10% of total annual parking demand," verified by annual parking-count data) or delete the low-usage exception entirely and retain only the Agricultural, Residential, and Stormwater exceptions.

5. **Item 20:** Add an express **fencing standard** (minimum height, type, maintenance) and a **night-containment requirement** for all livestock (modeled on the existing chicken ordinance at §6.08.020(C)(3)), plus an **escapement/recapture plan** and **indemnification of the City** for livestock-on-highway collisions.

6. **Item 29:** Require an **annual actuarial review** of the stormwater account's funding gap before any transfer, so that the transfer is driven by engineering need, not surplus availability.

7. **Item 31:** Add a **prepayment trigger** — if SPOT collections exceed projections, the loan shall be prepaid within 60 days, not held to the 4-year cap.

### Systemic

8. **cheyennecity.org:** Immediately fix the Council Agendas page to link to the **live Granicus portal** and archive all current-year PDFs. Designate a web-accessibility coordinator to bring the portal into WCAG 2.1 AA compliance.

9. **WARM governance:** Consider appointing an **independent risk professional** (outside the Compliance Department) to the WARM JPB, or at minimum, require the Compliance Director to recuse from WARM board votes that directly affect the City's own loss history.

10. **Ordinance-coordination review:** Before Items 18–21 advance, the City Attorney should conduct a **cross-reference audit** to confirm that the four ordinances, taken together, do not create internal code conflicts (e.g., Item 18's grandfather clause in §2.2.3 vs. §1.3.11; Item 20's "continued livestock upon annexation" duplicating Item 18's grandfather clause).

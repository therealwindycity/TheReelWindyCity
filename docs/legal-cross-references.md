# Legal authority and passage cross-references

The legal panel accompanies indexed agenda items on meeting pages and in the interactive workspace. Seven January 26, 2026 entries have manually researched **partial** reviews; the full January agenda includes subitems. Other entries receive **automated screening**, not a completed legal audit. The screening extracts explicitly written Wyoming statute numbers and presents candidate ordinance-passage checks for council ordinance entries. It does not independently establish applicability, research all governing authorities or historical versions, obtain every final enactment record, determine whether every requirement was satisfied, or automatically establish illegalities. Those limitations remain visible on the site. An absent citation is not evidence that no law applies. Agenda items and scheduled action are not proof of passage.

## Automatic discovery and publication

On the default branch, the hourly Pages workflow restores a GitHub Actions cache, assembles the meeting index, and attempts 12 agenda sources per run, with three concurrent workers. Never-attempted sources are prioritized, then the least recently attempted, so inaccessible sources cannot permanently block the rest of the queue. It uses known official Cheyenne agenda links first and otherwise PDF filenames containing `agenda` from allowlisted archive repositories. Both the meeting date and the governing body must match the source heading, and the existing extraction validator requires unique item IDs, nonempty text, and verbatim source quotations. Where Granicus HTML supports it, lettered subitems are recovered separately and cross-checked against the validated top-level list. PDF extraction can combine subitems. Each published extraction records the source, content hash, date and limitation. Failed attempts retain the last good data for the same requested source and report the error; changed URLs cannot silently reuse the old record. Missing sources and extraction failures remain visible as gaps. The cache is best-effort and can expire; the queue starts over if lost. Website publication follows the usual Pages deployment; no credentials or paid API are needed.

This discovery operates on the Cheyenne meeting index and known archived agenda links, not every government in Wyoming. It cannot process image-only PDFs without a text layer, discover documents not represented in the index, or guarantee extraction of every subitem. It automatically fetches indexed minutes and linked Granicus supporting documents and links cautious text matches to their source, but does not automatically obtain every signed ordinance, notice, publication proof or other case-specific record. Consequently it does **not** finish the requested substantive legal audit on its own. Online availability, version-at-time, local procedure and exceptions require additional reliable data and legal analysis; there is no guaranteed automated finding or deadline. Do not treat either silence or an unverified result as compliance or a violation.

The generated website files `src/data/legal-audit-generated.json` and `src/data/legal-evidence-generated.json`, and `src/data/legal-support-generated.json` are ignored and reconstructed by prebuild/predev/pretypecheck. The caches live at `.sync/legal-audit-cache.json` and `.sync/legal-evidence-cache.json`, and `.sync/legal-support-cache.json`. Run `npm run data:assemble && npm run data:legal-audit && npm run data:legal-evidence && npm run data:legal-support` to try bounded live refreshes; ordinary builds do not contact agenda or minutes sources. Tests exercise real source fixtures, date/body guards, queue ordering, failure retention, archive URL restrictions, and motion parsing.

## Supporting documents and fiscal review

For Granicus HTML agendas, the extractor associates each `Document` link with the preceding uniquely indexed item or subitem in source order and rejects unapproved URLs. A separate hourly queue attempts up to 16 distinct linked documents with four workers. It checks redirects and size, extracts PDF text, retains SHA-256 and capture time, lists explicitly cited Wyoming statutes, and shows bounded source excerpts mentioning notice, hearings, publication, enactment, or funding. Extracted excerpts are whitespace-normalized keyword leads, **not** evidence that a requirement was satisfied; the full document may be a proposal, legal text or a staff assertion rather than an independent notice or an adopted enactment. Failed requests retain last-good extraction, are displayed, and rotate behind unattempted sources. Links and extracted text match only the same meeting, item number and exact item description. Documents not linked in the indexed agenda, image-only PDFs, and misidentified or missing source material remain gaps. The data cache is best-effort and may be evicted.

The bundled `2026-01-26-warrants.pdf` is the January 8 fire department **policy brief** (the agenda’s Additional Information link), not the text of the proposed or enacted ordinance. It is identified accordingly; the guided session links the proposed ordinance to its actual official URL rather than substituting the policy brief. The January 26 budget reappropriation review compares the treasurer’s proposed packet, recorded Resolution 6508 vote, and W.S. §§ 16-4-112–113. A $1 difference between the packet’s rounded proposed appropriation and revenue totals is a reconciliation lead, possibly rounding, not an established violation. Final text and unrounded ledger data remain necessary.

## Automated evidence matching

The same hourly workflow attempts 12 minutes sources, with an independently rotating queue. Sources are allowlisted; redirects are checked at each hop, downloads are bounded, and the body/date in the document must match. A bundled machine-extracted set of 2026 minutes is used offline only where its date and Granicus clip exactly match the indexed meeting. Failure to fetch never erases the last good same-source record. Source links, failure details, and candidate excerpts appear on the item panel.

Ordinance descriptions are matched against individual ordinance blocks in minutes from the same governing body within 180 days, using a high subject-text similarity threshold and refusing ambiguous matches. A carried approval is reported only when a matching reading motion is followed by an unambiguous motion carried, or the main motion is explicitly recorded carried; postponements and amendment motions are not treated as final passage. Other items can receive same-meeting exact-phrase matches, but those are expressly text mentions, not vote findings. Source text and identity need verification, especially where near-identical measures recur; a minute may be an imperfect machine transcription. A candidate first-reading and carried final-approval interval under ten days is flagged for follow-up under W.S. 15-1-115(c), not adjudicated as illegal: statutory history, exceptions, identity, the precise actions, and the complete official record must be checked. Absence of a match or an evidence download failure is not evidence of noncompliance. The automated checks do not establish that all required readings occurred, the voting threshold was met, the law was published, or the ordinance took effect.

## Maintaining a manual review

Edit `src/data/legal-cross-references.json`. Use the exact meeting ID, agenda item number and a distinctive text guard from the actual agenda. Matching requires all three; unrelated meetings or changed text fail closed. For each item record the date researched, authoritative links (including the version of the law effective on the action date), and separate requirements. A requirement needs the legal citation, accurate rule and applicability, finding and its limitations, next records to obtain, and links to case-specific evidence. Ordinances, resolutions, contracts, public hearings, consent items, and committees do not all have the same requirements. The legal checklist is not exhaustive; municipal, state, federal and historical law may all be relevant.

Statuses are **unverified**, **partial**, **supported**, **conflicting**, and **not-applicable**. Missing records mean unverified, not failure or compliance; use partial when evidence addresses only part of the requirement. Supported, conflicting and not-applicable require case-specific evidence and an explanation. Never equate an agenda or council vote alone with a signed ordinance, effective date, publication, or full procedural compliance. Retain contrary evidence, identify sources and distinguish proposed from enacted language. Verify statutory amendments, exceptions, local charter provisions, meeting date and the item's actual identity before assigning a requirement.
